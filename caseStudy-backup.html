01

203 Versions to Get One Right.

The Challenge

A client needed a standee that was heavier, more stable, and more durable than anything in the market. Every off-the-shelf option failed their requirement.

What We Did

We didn't guess. We iterated. Our team developed and tested 203 different variations — adjusting weight, base structure, material thickness, and stability until we had a version that met the client's specific use case. Each variant was presented back for evaluation.

The Result

A custom heavy-duty standee built for long-term, high-stability deployment. The client got a product that actually solved their problem — not a standard product with a new sticker.

Customisation
02

We Repaired a Competitor's Stock.

The Challenge

A customer came to us in trouble. A shipment of standees from a competitor had arrived damaged. The original factory refused to take the stock back or offer replacements. The customer was looking at a significant write-off.

What We Did

The damaged product wasn't ours. The customer wasn't ours. But they needed help, so we took the work on as a service gesture. Our team examined the damaged stock and successfully repaired around 70% of the units.

The Result

The customer recovered most of the value of the damaged shipment. They did, however, remember who picked up the phone when their own supplier wouldn't. They buy from us now.

Service Attitude
03

6,000 Dented Standees. Not Our Fault. We Took Them Back Anyway.

The Challenge

A large order of approximately 6,000 standees was damaged during transit on the way to Chennai. The dents were caused by handling — not by our manufacturing or packaging.

What We Did

Strictly speaking, transit damage is the transporter's problem. But the client needed the product. We took back the damaged stock, coordinated transportation from both ends, and resolved the shipment issue.

The Result

The client got reliable support during a difficult, large-scale logistics failure. They learned what our replacement commitment actually means when tested at scale.

After-Sales Reliability
04

An Ultra-Thin Glass Frame. Eighteen Inches by Three.

The Challenge

A supermarket client needed a display frame for a very specific shelf dimension — 18 inches by 3 inches — with an ultra-thin profile. Nothing in the standard catalogue worked. Most manufacturers don't make glass frames at this size and thickness.

What We Did

We engineered the frame from scratch. Ultra-thin profile, custom-cut glass, precision finishing — built to fit the exact shelf dimension without wasting an inch of display space.

The Result

A display frame that fit the shelf perfectly and looked clean enough to sit on the supermarket floor. The client got a product built for their real-world constraint, not a standard product they had to work around.

Custom Engineering
05

A Chrome Finish at a Non-Chrome Price.

The Challenge

A client wanted the premium look of chrome-finished standees on the side profile — but the cost of real chrome was outside their budget.

What We Did

We didn't compromise on the finish — we re-engineered the process. Our team developed a side chrome-like appearance using anodised material and specific process changes. The result visually matches chrome finish while costing significantly less to produce.

The Result

The client got the premium brand presentation they wanted without the premium price tag. Same visual impact, better margins.

Premium Finishes, Smarter Cost
06

18 Hours of Battery. For Displays That Cannot Go Dark.

The Challenge

A client operated in environments where power outages were frequent and branded displays going dark mid-shift was unacceptable. Standard battery tabletops offered 4–6 hours of backup — nowhere near enough.

What We Did

We redesigned the power architecture to deliver 18 hours of backup — enough to survive a full shift plus a full outage without plugging in. We also developed a no-battery variant for budget-sensitive customers who wanted the form factor without the battery system.

The Result

The client got displays that stayed lit when they needed them most. The catalogue got two new variants — premium battery-backed, and no-battery — built to serve completely different customer segments.

Engineering for Reality
